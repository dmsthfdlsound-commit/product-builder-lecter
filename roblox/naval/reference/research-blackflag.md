# 블랙 플래그 해상전 메커니즘 분해

> Assassin's Creed IV: Black Flag naval combat — mechanics teardown for a Roblox "captain of a ship" port. NOTE ON SOURCES: search results in Aug 2026 are heavily polluted by "AC Black Flag Resynced" (the 2026 remake). Where a mechanic differs between the 2013 original and Resynced, I flag it. Numbers from Resynced guides are marked as such and should be treated as directional, not as the original's tuning.

## There are exactly three sail states — anchored/sails-down, half sail, full sail — and the whole handling model is a two-axis tradeoff between them (speed vs. turn radius).
Full sail is fast but reduces maneuverability; half sail lets you 'make tight turns in the open sea or around obstacles.' Dropping sails entirely kills speed and lets you pivot almost in place — one guide notes 'at full sail this maneuver is nearly impossible, but with sails down it happens quickly and cleanly,' and that turning ability improves further when the anchor drops. Some guides describe a third 'travel sail' tier; I could not confirm that for the 2013 original (it is a documented AC Rogue feature), so treat 3 states as the safe model.

**→ 시사점**: Ship a 3-state discrete throttle, not an analog one. Discrete states are the reason the feel survives: each state is a COMMITMENT the player can feel and name ('I'm going in at half sail'), it gives you 3 clean animation/audio/camera presets to author instead of a continuum to blend, it maps to one button pair (up/down through states) which is essential for Roblox mobile, and it makes the speed↔turn tradeoff a legible decision rather than a mushy gradient. Analog throttle would let players sit at a local optimum forever; discrete states force a choice every engagement. LOAD-BEARING.

## Sail state is secretly a whole-game state machine, not just a speed setting — it switches camera, audio, crew animation and even what the game is 'about' in that moment.
At full sail in open water the camera pulls back, spray and camera sway increase, and the crew starts singing one of 35 collectible sea shanties; at half sail near enemies the framing tightens into combat. Resynced explicitly removed 'camera sway during naval gameplay' by default and made screen shake toggleable — and the original had a popular Nexus mod, 'Stable Camera – Remove Camera Shake,' which tells you how aggressive the original's shake was.

**→ 시사점**: Bind camera FOV/distance/shake, water spray, wind SFX volume, shanty playback and crew idle animations to the sail state enum. One state change should fire ~6 simultaneous feedback channels. This is cheap to build in Roblox and is probably the single highest juice-per-dev-hour item in the whole port. Also: ship an accessibility toggle for shake/sway from day one — Ubisoft learned this the hard way. LOAD-BEARING (the state-machine coupling), OPTIONAL (the specific sway curve).

## In the 2013 original, wind is mostly READOUT and theatre — the actual speed effect is small and community-contested. The load-bearing part is the minimap arrow that changes colour, not the physics.
The minimap shows a wind arrow that goes green → yellow → red depending on your heading relative to it, and official-ish guidance says to 'maintain a high speed by always sailing with or across it, rather than against it.' But players who dug into it report wind has 'very little effect in the base game — there are no noticeable speed differences when tacking or sailing into the wind,' and a realism-mod thread states that at full speed you go as fast into the wind as with it. Another player describes a real alignment multiplier (headwind slows, 90° neutral, tailwind speeds up). CONFLICTING — I would not assert a specific multiplier. Resynced (2026) explicitly made wind 'a visible, dynamic factor' and 'wind direction determines combat approach,' i.e. Ubisoft themselves judged the original's wind too weak.

**→ 시사점**: Do NOT chase realistic sailing physics. Instead: (a) implement a modest, honest alignment multiplier — something like 1.15x tailwind / 1.0x beam / 0.8x headwind is a reasonable starting point to tune, since the original's magnitude is unknown; and (b) invest disproportionately in the READOUT — a colour-coded wind arrow the player can read in 100ms. The positioning decision comes from the readout plus the fact that wind reorients over time, so a chase that starts favourable turns unfavourable. Keep the penalty small enough that a player who ignores wind still has fun; wind should be a skill expression, not a tax. LOAD-BEARING (the arrow/readout), OPTIONAL DEPTH (the physics fidelity).

## The real 'wind gameplay' in Black Flag is weather as a third combatant: rogue winds, rogue waves, waterspouts, storms and lightning that damage everyone, including the player.
Rogue winds will shove a full-sail ship into obstacles even if you steer hard the other way — the documented counter is to drop to half sail or alternate between states. Rogue waves are survived by pointing the bow directly into them. Storms create real strategic choices, e.g. whether to stay inside a storm for concealment from a pursuing fleet or leave and expose yourself to fire. Players can deliberately lure enemies into bad conditions. Resynced added two waterspout types and lightning that does AoE damage in water.

**→ 시사점**: This is the best value-for-money 'wind' feature: a periodic environmental hazard is far cheaper to build than a sailing sim and produces better stories. In Roblox terms: a roaming storm volume that (1) forces sail-state changes because full sail becomes uncontrollable, (2) spawns telegraphed AoE hazards, (3) reduces render/nametag distance so it doubles as a stealth/escape tool. Note the elegance: the hazard's counter IS the core mechanic (drop to half sail / face the wave head-on), so weather teaches sailing. LOAD-BEARING as a concept, OPTIONAL in quantity — one storm type and one wave type is enough for v1.

## The arsenal is DIRECTIONAL by design — each face of the ship fires something different — which is what converts combat from an aim duel into a positioning/driving game.
Broadside cannons and heavy shot fire from the sides, chase cannons from the bow, fire barrels drop astern, the ram works only on the prow ('only the prow deals damage, as collisions along the ship's sides do not'), mortars fire indirectly anywhere. As one guide puts it, 'the Jackdaw's arsenal is directional, with each side of the ship firing something different, which is the foundation of every good naval fight,' and consequently 'U-turns are one of the most important tactics of combat... because weapons on the Jackdaw are locked behind directional use.' Lore/wiki stat for flavour: 46 broadside cannons, 4 chasers, 2 swivel guns.

**→ 시사점**: THIS is the mechanical heart and the thing most Roblox ship games miss. Do not give the player a turret. Bolt every weapon to a firing arc. The result: the primary verb of combat becomes DRIVING, the ship becomes the weapon, and the fantasy 'I am a captain' emerges from mechanics rather than from a hat. It also solves mobile input — you steer, and the arc decides which weapon is legal, so you need fewer buttons. LOAD-BEARING, non-negotiable.

## Broadside round shot = the unlimited, aimed, arcing baseline. It is the only weapon with no ammo cost, and it is aimed with a manual trajectory reticle.
Round shot is 'the basic long-ranged weapon' fired from the sides; you hold aim and 'determine the attack's direction and correct the projectiles' trajectory using the right analog stick.' Ammunition is not limited the way heavy shot and mortars are.

**→ 시사점**: Give players exactly one infinite weapon so a fight can never soft-lock, and make it the one that requires manual arc/lead aiming so mastery has somewhere to go. Every other weapon should be scarce. The infinite/scarce split is what makes players think. LOAD-BEARING.

## Heavy shot = close-range, unaimed, limited-ammo burst. Its whole design purpose is to make CLOSING THE DISTANCE a decision with teeth.
Heavy shot 'needs to be fired at close range,' is fired 'without aiming' by simply facing your broadside at the target and pressing fire, delivers instant burst damage, and 'the number of heavy shots you have is heavily limited' (raised by upgrades). There's no trajectory control, so 'firing them requires quite a lot of skill at maneuvering the ship.' Guides call it ideal as an opening alpha strike or a finisher.

**→ 시사점**: This is a beautifully cheap piece of design: remove the aiming skill, and the skill relocates to DRIVING. Port it verbatim — a no-aim, big-damage, short-range, low-ammo broadside. It creates the game's best risk moment: getting close is where you're most likely to eat a return broadside. Pairs with brace (below) to create the signature exchange: sail in, brace their volley, dump heavy shot. LOAD-BEARING.

## Mortars = indirect fire with a free-roaming world-space impact reticle, long range, scarce ammo. It is the only weapon that ignores firing arcs, and the only one that requires target LEADING.
Mortars 'don't fire from any specific side, instead dropping from the sky.' You aim a small circle whose centre is the impact point on the water; effective use requires leading a moving target ('track its movement pattern, project where it will be when your mortar lands, and aim there'). Ammo is limited and upgraded via mortar storage. It's the tool for softening escorts before they can respond and for static targets like fort towers. Forts return the favour: incoming mortars are telegraphed as 'large orange circles on the water' you must steer out of.

**→ 시사점**: Two things to steal. (1) The telegraphed ground circle is the cleanest possible AoE readout and is trivially implementable in Roblox — a decal/part on the water with a fill animation. Use it for BOTH directions (player mortar aim preview and incoming enemy fire). (2) Indirect fire is the answer to 'what do I do when I'm out of arc?' — without it, being out of position feels like dead time. Constrain it with scarce ammo and a long flight time so it never replaces broadsides. LOAD-BEARING (indirect option + ground telegraph). Server-authoritative impact resolution is mandatory in Roblox or this becomes the top exploit.

## Swivel gun = a precision, low-damage weapon whose ONLY real job is to convert 'weak points' into burst damage. It is a mini-game layered on top of the HP bar.
Sustained cannon fire makes weak points appear on enemy ships, 'visualized by red animated cross icons.' The swivel gun is a hold-to-charge, release-to-fire aimed shot: you hold the button, a crosshair drifts toward the target marker, and you release when they align, blasting the weak point open for 'a significant chunk of damage.' Secondary uses: sniping individual enemy crew, shooting powder barrels on the deck of a crippled ship, and detonating fire barrels in the water before you sail into them. The Elite/Ultimate upgrade makes it fire fully automatically at all weak points with no manual aim.

**→ 시사점**: Steal the STRUCTURE: your bread-and-butter weapon generates targets that a second, precision weapon cashes in. This is a damage multiplier gated on attention, so skilled players kill ~30-40% faster without any stat difference — the best kind of skill expression for a Roblox game with mixed player ability. The hold-and-release alignment input is also mobile-friendly (one touch, timed release). Note the upgrade path: the top tier AUTOMATES the mini-game — that's a smart reward, converting mastery into convenience. LOAD-BEARING (weak-point → precision-weapon loop), OPTIONAL (the specific charge-align input).

## Fire barrels = rear-facing area denial. They exist purely to make being CHASED a playable state instead of a losing state.
Fire barrels are dropped astern; 'if a pursuer sails directly into them, the barrels detonate and deal heavy damage.' Documented combo: lay barrels in the enemy's path, then mortar them to force them into the trap. Counterplay exists — you can shoot enemy barrels with the swivel gun before hitting them.

**→ 시사점**: Every arc needs a verb. Without a rear weapon, 'I'm being chased' is dead time and the player just holds full sail. With one, retreating becomes an aggressive act. Also note the herding combo (barrels + mortar) — that's emergent depth from two simple systems, which is exactly the kind of depth you want because you don't have to author it. And the counterplay (shoot the barrel) means the tool isn't degenerate. LOAD-BEARING (a rear-arc verb), the specific 'barrel' fantasy is OPTIONAL.

## Ram = the melee option. Bow-only, requires full sail to build speed, and is the fast-clear answer to small ships.
'Raise the Jackdaw's sails to full to build up speed, then strike the enemy ship head-on with the bow.' Only the prow deals damage. Ramming is innate (not purchased) but scales with upgrades, and once upgraded lets you 'disable smaller ships such as Schooners and Brigs quickly at close range.' Legendary ship El Impoluto is built around ram-dashing YOU, so the game demonstrates the mechanic from the receiving end.

**→ 시사점**: Include a ram: it's the only weapon whose input is 'drive well,' it makes full sail tactically relevant inside combat (otherwise full sail is just travel), and it gives players a satisfying answer to trash mobs so fights against small ships don't drag. Also note the ram is the one weapon that requires a specific sail state — that's how you keep all three states live during combat. LOAD-BEARING (it closes the loop between sail states and combat).

## The 'rock-paper-scissors' is NOT a damage-type triangle — it's a ROLE MATRIX across three axes: range band × firing arc × ammo scarcity. Nothing hard-counters anything; each tool owns a situation.
Mapping it out: mortar = long range / any arc / very scarce / slow. Round shot = mid range / side arc / infinite / aimed. Chase cannons = mid range / bow arc / for pursuit or fleeing. Heavy shot = short range / side arc / scarce / no aim / burst. Ram = contact / bow arc / free / needs full sail. Fire barrels = behind you / scarce / area denial. Swivel gun = any range / precision / consumes only attention / only works on weak points. Against a Man-o'-War, guides call the swivel gun 'the most powerful tool' — not because it does the most damage, but because weak points are the only efficient way through heavy armour. La Dama Negra is armoured such that hitting the bow or stern is required, i.e. the enemy design forces arc changes.

**→ 시사점**: Design your weapon table on those three axes explicitly and check that every cell is covered. The player should never be able to answer 'what do I press' without first answering 'where am I, how far, and what do I have left.' Crucially, enemy DESIGN is what activates the matrix — armour that's only weak at bow/stern, escorts that must be cleared at range, rammers that punish you for sitting still. Build 4-5 enemy archetypes that each invalidate one weapon, or your beautiful arsenal collapses into 'always broadside.' LOAD-BEARING: the axes and the enemy archetypes. OPTIONAL: having all 7 weapons at launch — 4 (broadside, heavy, mortar, swivel) is a complete game.

## Damage model: a SEGMENTED health bar (reported as 3 segments) with out-of-combat auto-repair that refills only up to the top of the segment you're currently in.
'The Jackdaw's health bar is split into three segments.' Crew 'repair minor damage automatically whenever you are not actively engaged in combat,' but 'they'll refill the ship's health, but only up to the top of whichever health bar segment you're currently in.' Full restoration needs a Harbourmaster visit or a successful boarding. (Segment count is best-documented in Resynced guides; the segmented-bar concept is consistent with the original.)

**→ 시사점**: This is a superb, portable mechanic and I'd rank it top-3 for the Roblox port. It gives you forgiveness (no trip back to port after every scratch) AND stakes (losing a whole segment is permanent until you earn a real repair) AND a legible 'lives' readout that reads at a glance on a phone screen. Psychologically it's loss-aversion with a floor: players will fight hard to avoid dropping a segment, which is a much stronger motivator than a continuous bar. Roblox-specific bonus: it lets you avoid a punishing death-loop without adding regen that trivializes combat. LOAD-BEARING.

## BRACE is the defensive read that makes the combat a duel: enemy gun ports glow orange for about a second before firing, and holding the brace button through the volley massively reduces damage.
'Enemy cannons light up for a second right before shooting'; 'when you see an enemy ship's ports glow orange, you can hold the block button to brace.' Brace is hold X/Square on console, Spacebar on PC, and guides describe it as 'at the heart of naval battles... the game's way of ensuring that incoming damage is reduced, allowing you to have a chance at fighting back in the most dire of situations.' (Sources attribute brace to AC IV; note that most current write-ups are Resynced-era, and Resynced added a 'perfect brace' timing variant via an officer perk, which is a remake addition.)

**→ 시사점**: Port this FIRST. A ~1s glowing telegraph plus a hold-to-mitigate input is the entire reason Black Flag's naval combat feels like combat rather than an HP race. It gives the player something to DO while under fire, converts incoming damage from 'unfair' to 'my fault,' and creates the skill gradient with zero stat inflation. It is also almost free to implement in Roblox (emissive material swap on gunport parts + a server-validated damage-reduction flag). Consider a 'perfect brace' bonus window for a mastery layer. LOAD-BEARING, highest priority.

## Emptying an enemy's health bar does NOT sink it — it CRIPPLES it. A grapple icon appears above the ship and the player then chooses: board for roughly double loot, or sink it for speed.
'Pound an enemy vessel with the Jackdaw's cannons until its health bar empties, watch for the grappling hook icon to appear above the ship, which signals it's ready to board,' with a 'Press B to Board' prompt. 'After combat, you'll have two options available with a disabled ship. If you're in a hurry, sink it immediately, but if you have the time to earn extra loot, come in close for a boarding party.' Boarding yields 'double what you'd get by simply destroying them' and gives you everything the spyglass showed when you scouted it, whereas sinking gives 'only a fraction.' Failing to board before the ship sinks forfeits rare material and recruit drops.

**→ 시사점**: This single design decision is the engine of the whole loop, and it's the thing most naval games get wrong by making 0 HP = death. Steal it exactly. Benefits: (1) it converts the kill from a full stop into a DECISION, and decisions are what players remember; (2) it creates a natural greed/risk gate — boarding takes time, and in a multi-ship fight time is dangerous; (3) it gives you a diegetic place to hang the reward menu. Add a visible sinking timer on the crippled ship so the greed decision has a clock. LOAD-BEARING, top-3.

## During a boarding, YOUR ship becomes invulnerable — all incoming damage from every source is nullified. Boarding is therefore simultaneously a loot event, a full heal, and a safe reset from a losing fight.
'After you issue the order to seize the enemy vessel, the Jackdaw becomes invulnerable to damage, the remaining ships deal no damage. An approach like this means that, with a bit of thought, you could repair your ship during the battle. It only takes seizing the immobilized ship, decide to make the repairs and voila! After you resume fighting, your health bar will be back to full.' Resynced kept this: 'when boarding a ship, every source of damage gets nullified, including mortar shots, incoming ships, and even waterspouts.'

**→ 시사점**: This is the game's comeback mechanic and it's disguised as a reward. It means a fight against 3 ships is never a death spiral — cripple the weakest, board it, heal, re-enter. Emotionally it's the peak: maximum tension right before, total safety and reward right after, which is a textbook tension-release curve. For Roblox this is doubly valuable because it gives you a legitimate reason to freeze ship physics/networking during the boarding sequence (huge technical simplification — you can hard-dock the two hulls with a weld and stop simulating). LOAD-BEARING and technically convenient. Watch the exploit: rate-limit it or players will chain-board to become unkillable.

## Boarding objectives are a fixed ritual that scales by ship class: kill 5 crew (Schooner), 10 (Brig), 15 (Frigate), 20 (Man-o'-War) — plus 1 random secondary for Frigates and 2 for Man-o'-Wars.
Primary objective is always a crew-kill count: 5/10/15/20 by class. Secondary objectives are randomly drawn from: kill the Captain, kill the officers (two), kill the scouts (two riflemen up the masts), blow up the powder reserves, or cut down the flag. Enemies stream up onto the deck through hatches and your own sailors join the fight. Players do complain: a well-known Steam thread is titled 'Boarding enemy ships seems tedious because it's the same thing every time?'

**→ 시사점**: Two lessons, one positive one negative. POSITIVE: a short, identical, learnable ritual whose LENGTH scales with prize size is exactly right — the player always knows what boarding costs, so the greed decision is clean, and bigger ships feel bigger because the ritual is longer. Randomized secondaries are a cheap variety generator (5 objectives from 1 authored system). NEGATIVE: even with that, Black Flag's boarding went stale, and that's with a full melee combat system behind it. For Roblox, plan a boarding mini-game of 20-45 seconds max, with at least 4-6 secondary variants and objectives that use DIFFERENT verbs (a timed one, a stealth one, a find-the-thing one), not just 'kill N.' LOAD-BEARING (the scaling ritual), and the repetition risk is the #1 thing to design against.

## The post-boarding screen is a CHOICE OF REWARD, not an automatic payout: repair the ship, reduce your wanted level, add crew, or send the captured vessel to Kenway's Fleet — plus loot chests and the captain's lockbox.
After a successful boarding you 'can loot chests, recruit optional officers, and choose repair or morale bonus,' and options include using the ship to repair, to lower your wanted level, to seize the captain's chest, or 'Send to Kenway's Fleet' for ships you want to keep. Crew is a real soft resource: crew die in storms and boardings, 'forcing Edward to continuously recruit more members,' and you top up by recruiting in taverns, rescuing pirates (kill all the guards before the pirates die and the survivors join you), or after a boarding.

**→ 시사점**: Never auto-grant the reward. A 3-4 option choice at the moment of triumph does enormous work: it makes the player author their own run ('I'm hurt, I'll take repair'), it makes the same content produce different stories, it teaches the resource economy without a tutorial, and it turns one loot event into a decision the player will second-guess and remember. This is very cheap to build in Roblox — it's a UI panel — and disproportionately valuable. LOAD-BEARING. Crew-as-consumable is OPTIONAL depth, but it's what makes 'repair vs crew' a real dilemma, so I'd keep a light version.

## The upgrade tree is a wide, shallow ladder over ~10 systems, double-gated by (a) a shop-tier unlock and (b) world-found blueprints — so money alone can never finish it.
Upgradeable at Harbourmasters: Hull Armour, Broadside Cannons, Round Shot Strength, Heavy Shot Strength, Mortars, Mortar/ammo Storage, Swivel Guns, Ram, Chase Cannons, plus storage and the Diving Bell. Currencies are Reales plus crafting materials — Metal, Wood, Cloth — that come off boarded ships. Gate 1: the Harbourmaster at Great Inagua has 3 levels, and 'each one opens a new batch of ship tiers'; top tiers stay locked until it's maxed. Gate 2: the final tier of each weapon needs a unique blueprint (called Elite Plans in 2013, Ultimate Plans in Resynced) — 12 of them, roughly half from underwater shipwrecks and half from treasure maps. Resynced's full-completion cost is quoted at ~315,800 Reales, 4,755 Metal, 2,750 Wood, 550 Cloth, 12 plans; the 2013 figures were in a similar range but I can't confirm them exactly, so treat these as directional. Community consensus on priority: Hull Armour first, then Broadside Cannons.

**→ 시사점**: Copy the double gate. Currency-only progression gets solved by grinding and then feels hollow; blueprint gating forces EXPLORATION, which is how the upgrade tree ends up pulling players through your world instead of through your shop. Wide-and-shallow (10 systems × 4 tiers) also beats deep-and-narrow because every purchase changes how the ship plays in a felt way, and because you can always afford *something*, so the shop is never a wall. Note that community priority converges on survivability first — expect and design for that. LOAD-BEARING: two currencies (soft + materials from boarding) and a non-purchasable gate. OPTIONAL: the exact tier counts.

## Content gating is done with SHIP LEVELS surfaced through the spyglass — white = you can take it, red = it will kill you — with regions escalating from north to south. It's a soft, self-enforced difficulty curve, not a locked door.
'When you use your telescope on a ship it shows their level — those shown in red are a higher level than your ship. Those in white are ships you should be able to attack and defeat. As you upgrade the Jackdaw your level will increase allowing you to challenge higher level ships.' ## The spyglass also does pre-commitment work: it shows you the cargo BEFORE the fight, and boarding is what pays out exactly what you were shown.

It converts a random reward into a goal. It also makes the board-vs-sink decision honest, because the player knows precisely what sinking forfeits. ## The economy is a four-layer loop: opportunistic piracy → posted contracts → convoys/forts → a passive fleet meta-game, and forts are the key that unlocks the fleet's higher-paying regions.
~15 Naval Contracts are posted side jobs (sink X, protect Y, gather Z materials) paying roughly 1,200-2,400 Reales base plus a bonus objective (Resynced figures). Naval Convoys require boarding and commandeering the largest lead ship and pay well via the Captain's Lockbox. Kenway's Fleet is the passive layer: captured ships are docked and sent on timed real-world-minutes trade routes from the Captain's Cabin, returning Reales and materials; ship class determines mission type (Schooners trade, Brigs pirate, Frigates scout, Man-o'-Wars patrol, where patrols reduce a route's risk and protect your other ships). Critically, 'to access new trade regions, you must systematically capture the naval forts' — take a fort and that whole area of sea opens for your fleet with better payouts. Legendary ships pay a lump sum (quoted around 12,000 Reales in Resynced; the 2013 payouts were reported higher — uncertain) plus cosmetics. All Resynced income figures should be treated as directional.

**→ 시사점**: The architecture to steal: (1) always-available opportunistic income so nothing is ever mandatory; (2) posted contracts that give the aimless player a goal in one tap; (3) big-ticket set-pieces (convoys, forts) that require the full arsenal; (4) a passive layer that makes captured ships PERMANENTLY meaningful. Layer 4 is a perfect fit for Roblox's short-session, come-back-and-check-in behaviour, and it's monetizable without pay-to-win (sell berth slots and cosmetics, never route income). The fort→region unlock is the elegant bit: a combat achievement expands your ECONOMY, which ties the two loops together so neither can be ignored. LOAD-BEARING: layers 1, 2 and 4. Forts and legendary ships are OPTIONAL DEPTH for v2.

## Why 5 minutes is fun with zero progression: a complete, self-authored, ~2-minute dramatic arc is always available within seconds, and the game never shows you a number.
Synthesis across retrospectives: (a) you are always already in the vehicle and always already armed — no loadout, no queue, no loading screen between sea and combat; (b) targets are self-selected and pre-scouted with the spyglass, so the player sets their own difficulty and prize; (c) the arc is complete — approach, exchange under fire, cripple, board, loot — and the outcome is decided in roughly 90-180 seconds; (d) the failure state is soft (segmented health, boarding-as-heal), so risk is cheap to take; (e) the ambient layer (shanties, water, crew) is itself rewarding, which is why 3-hour 'just sailing' videos exist and why the game is described as therapeutic; (f) commentators specifically note the design 'benefits from disclosure — Black Flag never prints a number; players learn its odds by feel,' and that progression 'tied player progression to player behavior rather than to a currency shop — players got better by learning how to fight smarter, not by spending longer.' Note (f) is analyst framing rather than a developer statement.

**→ 시사점**: This is the acceptance test for the port: with all progression disabled, is one engagement still fun? Concretely — target time-to-first-cannonball under 30 seconds from spawn; a full engagement arc resolving in 90-180 seconds; the arc must be complete (a kill must END in a payoff moment, not fade out); difficulty must be player-selected via the spyglass rather than assigned; failure must cost minutes, not hours. And keep numbers off the screen — express damage through splinters, list, smoke and speed loss rather than floating combat text. On Roblox this matters more than anywhere else, because the median session is short and social and the player will judge you in the first two minutes. LOAD-BEARING: all of it. This is the actual product.

## ⛔ 하드 제약
- SOURCE HYGIENE: as of Aug 2026 almost all searchable AC4 naval content is about 'Black Flag Resynced' (2026 remake). Numbers I found for upgrade costs, contract payouts, fleet income and legendary rewards are Resynced figures and must be treated as directional only — do NOT hard-code them as balance targets. Mechanics confirmed for the 2013 original: 3 sail states, directional arsenal, round/heavy shot split, mortars, swivel gun + weak points, fire barrels, ram, brace, crippled-then-board, boarding invulnerability, 5/10/15/20 boarding kill counts, spyglass ship levels, Elite plan gating, 35 shanties, Kenway's Fleet.
- WIND IS NOT LOAD-BEARING IN THE ORIGINAL. Multiple credible player reports say the 2013 game's wind had little or no measurable speed effect; Resynced added real wind mechanics precisely because the original's were thin. Do not build a sailing simulator on the assumption Black Flag had one. Build the READOUT (colour-coded arrow) and a small tunable multiplier.
- ROBLOX PHYSICS: do not simulate buoyancy/hydrodynamics on multi-part hulls. Drive a single primary part (AlignPosition/AlignOrientation or a custom mover) with cosmetic geometry welded to it. Players standing on a fast-moving Roblox platform is the classic jitter/network-ownership failure; keep ship speeds modest, never teleport CFrames, and expect to hand network ownership of the hull to the helmsman.
- BOARDING INVULNERABILITY IS ALSO YOUR TECHNICAL ESCAPE HATCH — use it. Hard-dock the two hulls with a weld and stop simulating movement during the boarding sequence. Conversely it is an exploit surface: rate-limit boarding or players will chain-board to become unkillable.
- SERVER AUTHORITY: mortar indirect fire with a free-roaming world-space reticle, weak-point damage multipliers, and brace damage reduction must all resolve server-side. Client-authoritative damage in a PvP-capable naval game will be exploited within days.
- MOBILE IS THE MAJORITY PLATFORM. Two consequences: (1) the 7-weapon arsenal cannot map to 7 buttons — use firing arcs to context-switch so the same button does different things depending on heading; that is exactly why AC4's directional design works on touch. (2) Persistent cannon smoke, splinter particles and heavy post-FX will destroy mobile framerate; budget particles with LOD and ship shake/sway/blur as toggles from day one.
- AUDIO/IP: no AC assets, names, ship names, or music. Shanties must be original commissions that you own — Roblox audio privacy rules restrict uploaded audio above roughly 6 seconds to experiences owned by the uploader, so vocal loops need to be owned outright, not sourced.
- MONETIZATION: Black Flag's entire risk/reward engine depends on upgrades being earned through play, and analysts single this out as why it works ('progression tied to player behavior rather than to a currency shop'). If Robux can buy hull armour or weapon tiers, the loop collapses. Monetize cosmetics, fleet berth slots, and convenience — never combat stats and never route income.
- BOARDING REPETITION IS A KNOWN FAILURE MODE. Black Flag had a full melee combat system behind boarding and players still complained it was 'the same thing every time.' A thinner Roblox version will go stale faster. Cap the boarding sequence at 20-45 seconds and budget for 4-6 secondary objectives using genuinely different verbs — not just escalating kill counts.
- SESSION LENGTH: Roblox sessions are short and social. A 20-minute solo fort siege is not a v1 feature. The atomic unit must be a 90-180 second engagement that resolves completely.
- THE ARSENAL ONLY WORKS IF ENEMIES ACTIVATE IT. Without 4-5 enemy archetypes that each invalidate one weapon (armour weak only at bow/stern, rammers that punish stillness, escorts that must be cleared at range, chasers that force rear-arc play), every weapon collapses into 'always broadside' and the whole rock-paper-scissors is wasted work.

## 💡 기회
- Blox Fruits is the most-played game on Roblox (reported 500k+ concurrent) and is nominally a pirate game, but its ships are TRANSPORT — commentary notes players 'spend more time grinding levels and fighting bosses' than doing pirate things. The largest pirate-adjacent audience on the platform has no real naval combat system to play. That is the gap.
- Arcane Odyssey is the one Roblox game credited with weighty naval combat (wind, shot angle, sail damage — 'about being a better captain rather than just clicking the fastest'), but it is buried inside a large, slow RPG. Nobody on Roblox owns 'readable, 5-minute, arcade-weighty naval combat' as the PRIMARY product.
- BOARDING AS A REAL PLAYABLE SEQUENCE is essentially absent from Roblox naval games — they end fights with a sink and a loot popup. Cripple-then-choose-to-board, with a short deck fight and a choice-of-reward panel, is the single clearest differentiator available.
- THE BRACE TELEGRAPH IS UNCLAIMED. No Roblox naval game I could find uses a glowing-gunport read-and-mitigate defensive mechanic. It is cheap (emissive swap + a server flag), it reads perfectly on a phone, and it instantly makes combat feel like a duel instead of an HP race.
- SEGMENTED HEALTH WITH SEGMENT-FLOOR REGEN solves a problem every Roblox naval game has — either punishing repair trips or trivializing regen. Shipping it as the visible core readout is both a feel win and a UX win on small screens.
- SPYGLASS PRE-COMMITMENT (see the target's level, colour verdict and full cargo manifest BEFORE you commit) replaces an entire level-lock system with one piece of information, and turns random loot into a goal the player has already decided is theirs. Very cheap, and I saw nothing like it in the Roblox naval space.
- THE PASSIVE FLEET META is an unusually good fit for Roblox retention behaviour (leave, come back, collect) and monetizes cleanly via berth slots and cosmetics. Combine it with Black Flag's fort→region unlock so combat progress expands the economy, and you get two loops that feed each other — a structure Sea of Thieves-style social games explicitly lack (great crewing, no progression).
- SOCIAL MULTIPLIER BLACK FLAG NEVER HAD: Black Flag's crew are NPCs. On Roblox, real players in the crew roles (helm, guns, mortar, swivel, boarding party) plus the directional-arc design means positioning becomes a communication problem. That is a genuine improvement on the source material, not just a port — and it is what would make the 'captain of a ship' fantasy land harder than the original.
