# 적대적 검증: 재미/리텐션 (2차)

> Player-experience / retention critic — a Roblox player deciding in the first 5 minutes whether to close the app, and in 3 days whether to come back. Judged on felt fun and device reality (phone-majority, ~20-33 min sessions, low tolerance for input-does-nothing time), not on internal design coherence. Numbers below are recomputed from the document's own tables; platform figures are given as ranges because I could not verify them this session (web search budget exhausted, Reddit/devforum fetch blocked).

**판정: needs-rework**

## 🚫 The fun gate tests a loadout no new player ever receives. Gate B's vertical slice ships a "sloop" with 8-ish gun broadside, 3×1,000 HP, roundshot + heavy shot + brace. The actual shipped T1 sloop is 3 guns/side at 34 dmg, 3×420 HP, no heavy shot, no mortar, no fire barrel, no ram, and the spyglass is level-gated to Lv5.
**실패 시나리오**: Gate B returns PASS on a configuration that exists nowhere in the shipped game, the team locks the combat design as validated, and the real first session is: 3 guns, one weapon verb, no target information. Recompute the real first fight: 3 × 34 = 102/volley; at a plausible mobile hit rate of 40-50% that is ~45 effective; against the smallest listed enemy (600 HP cutter) that is roughly 60-70 seconds, and the T1 survivability table says the player should be fighting 1-2 of them, i.e. 120-140 seconds of holding an arc with three cannons and no spyglass to tell them what they are shooting at. The document's own failure threshold is 240s = HP sponge, and the new player is landing near it in the weakest possible state.

**처방**: Rebuild the slice around the literal shipped T1 sloop and re-run Gate B on it. Then fix the loadout rather than the test: give the T1 sloop the full weapon VOCABULARY in the first 10 minutes at tiny magnitude — 5-gun broadside, mortar with 1 shell, fire barrel with 1, ram enabled, swivel (already T1, keep it), and the spyglass at minute zero, not Lv5. Progression after that adds counts, ranges and multipliers, never new verbs. The seven-weapon RPS matrix is the entire fun thesis; a player who owns 1.5 of the seven is not playing the game you designed.

## 🚫 Spyglass is gated at Lv5 (~40-60 min by the doc's own XP curve) but the 5-minute onboarding script uses it at 0:12, and the whole "no level locks, we replace them with information" pillar depends on it.
**실패 시나리오**: The one session where a player most needs to know 'can I beat that thing' is the only session without the tool that answers it. New players will attack red-tier targets they cannot hurt, lose 60-140 seconds, and read the game as unfair — the exact failure the white/yellow/red system was invented to prevent. It also breaks the cargo-manifest desire loop, which the doc correctly identifies as its highest value-per-implementation-cost feature: without pre-fight manifests, loot becomes a random drop again.

**처방**: Spyglass at level 1, in the first 20 seconds, as the tutorial's first verb. Move whatever needs to live at Lv5 to something cosmetic. There is no version of this game where hiding target information from a beginner is correct.

## 🚫 T4 — the 31-40 hour goal and the gate on every top-tier module — requires 14 Sealed Plans from "wreck diving 7 + treasure maps 7," while the scope-defence section lists diving/underwater exploration as PERMANENTLY excluded and says plans will come from sea events instead.
**실패 시나리오**: The stated non-purchasable, exploration-only gate silently becomes an event-table RNG roll (weight 2-3 out of ~100 per 25s check). That converts the endgame from 'travel the world' into 'wait for a rare roll' — a pure grind wall with zero player agency, which is precisely the Dark Sea RNG-punishment pattern the doc names as the genre's worst failure. Players will hit T4 module unlocks, discover the blocker is a dice roll they cannot influence, and stop.

**처방**: Pick one and write it down: either diving ships in v1 as a 30-second minigame (not a new character controller — a fixed-camera timed interaction at a wreck), or plans come from a deterministic, visible, repeatable source (fortress capture, treasure-map chains bought with silver, contract-board rewards). Never leave a 30-hour gate behind a low-weight random roll.

## 🚫 The event-density guarantee is "any voyage meets at least one cargo target within 5 minutes," and drifting-cargo/wreck-debris (10s, trivial materials) carries 30% weight — the single most common event is the least interesting one.
**실패 시나리오**: A five-minute floor of empty ocean is a five-minute floor of players leaving. The doc itself says empty water reads as wasted time and targets ≤40s between reward and next contact — then sets the hard guarantee 7x looser than the target. Meanwhile ~35% of the interruptions that DO fire (30% floating box + 5% whale pod) are chores or scenery, so the felt gap between real engagements is far longer than the 25s roll implies.

**처방**: Hard guarantee: a combat-capable target within 60 seconds of the previous resolution, spawned off-screen at 900-1,100 studs so it reads as discovered rather than teleported in. Drop drifting cargo to 12% weight and make it a 2-second sail-through pickup with no stop, not a 10-second interaction. Anything that makes the player stop moving for a trickle reward should be deleted.

## 🚫 Brace — the named single differentiator — is a hold input that blocks firing and cuts speed 35%/turn 50%, keyed to a 1.1s telegraph with a 0.25s perfect window, read on a phone through ±7° wave roll, 0.35-0.70 camera shake, and your own gunsmoke occluding 40% of the screen by the document's own spec. The swivel gun requires hold-drift-release while steering authority is locked for 1.1s. Steady aim needs a 1.4s hold. All simultaneous, on two thumbs.
**실패 시나리오**: If brace success on mobile lands well under the 60%-within-3-fights target, the entire skill layer collapses: no duel feel, weak points rarely hit, and the claimed 1.8x mastery span evaporates. What remains is hold arc, tap fire, wait ~19 volleys, tap board. That is a slower Blox Fruits, and the differentiation pitch is gone. This is also the most expensive thing to discover late, because the enemy archetypes, TTK budget and progression multipliers are all tuned around brace existing.

**처방**: Test brace in isolation on a low-end Android in week 3, before any archetype work, with the smoke and shake at spec values. Ship a mobile affordance that does not depend on reading a 0.25s window through smoke: make the brace button itself the telegraph — full-screen edge pulse plus haptic at telegraph start, perfect window widened to 0.4s on touch input, and an auto-brace toggle that grants the -65% but never the -85%. Perfect brace should be a mastery bonus, never the baseline required to survive.

## 🚫 Summed dead-input time. 25s sinking cinematic on your own ship, 8-minute tow repair, 20s before auto-repair begins, 20s grounding, 2.5s non-skippable sail transitions with no S2→S0 (5s via two stages), 4s anchor drop/raise, 45s boarding cooldown, 45s+ full-stop deceleration on the largest hulls.
**실패 시나리오**: Each is individually justified as weight; together a session is riddled with 2-25 second windows where input does nothing. The 25-second sink cinematic is the worst: it seizes control at the exact moment the player just lost, on a phone, where app-switching is one swipe away. They will not come back mid-cinematic. Compounding multipliers make travel worse than advertised too — upwind 0.80 × full cargo 0.839 × two segments lost 0.78 ≈ 0.52x base, so a damaged loaded sloop beating upwind does ~48 studs/s, which is minutes of holding a slider. That is the sailing tax the doc claims to have solved, arriving through multiplier stacking.

**처방**: Sinking cinematic: 4 seconds, skippable after 1, and it ends in the dry-dock UI with the repair already paid or the insurance already spent — the player's next decision must be reachable within 6 seconds of death. Tow repair 8 min → 90 seconds. Auto-repair start 20s → 8s. Allow S2→S0 as a single 3s emergency 'drop sails' with a cost (crew stumble, 2s no fire). Floor total speed multiplier at 0.65x so no state combination produces a crawl.

## 🚫 There is no mechanical reason to bring a friend, and the shard density guarantees a lonely horizon. Solo is the balance baseline, party stat buffs are banned, loot is per-player instanced, officer passives stay at 100% when a human takes over, and every listed co-op reward is cosmetic or a ledger stamp. Meanwhile 6-8 simulated ships on an 8,000×8,000 field is roughly one ship per 8 million square studs.
**실패 시나리오**: Two failures at once. (1) The passenger seat is a worse game: the friend does not steer, does not pick targets, does not choose the reward, and gets teleported to an arena during boarding. Sea of Thieves works because bailing, repairing and mapping are urgent failure-critical human jobs; this design converted every one of those into an NPC passive and then hoped presence alone would be fun. (2) The entire cosmetic monetization thesis is 'the horizon is the canvas, your emblem is visible all session from 800 studs.' At that ship density you will almost never see another player's emblem, so the P1 price ladder (99-299 R$ emblems, 1,499-2,999 R$ flagship bundles) is priced on visibility the tech budget cannot deliver.

**처방**: Give the second seat one job that can FAIL and that the captain cannot do while steering: manual bilge pumping during flooding, or manual segment shoring, on a real timer with a real loss if ignored. One interdependent verb is worth more than every ledger stamp in the doc. Separately, cluster spawns: put a 2,000-stud-radius 'shipping lane' region that all players and events spawn into, so encounter density is a design parameter rather than a consequence of map size. If the horizon is going to sell cosmetics, players must be on the same stretch of horizon.

## 🚫 Nine parallel progression tracks and roughly 14 distinct numbers (silver/ducats — the doc uses both names for the same currency in different sections — timber, iron, canvas, sealed plans, voyage credits, voyage marks, XP, skill points, renown, notoriety, deeds, morale, loyalty), with the T5 endgame at 55-70 hours.
**실패 시나리오**: Ask a new player 'what are you working toward' and this document cannot produce a one-sentence answer. Games that retain on this platform have one nameable ladder. Worse, 55-70 hours against a D30 target of 4-6% means the T5 sidegrade endgame — the horizontal design the doc is proudest of — is built for a cohort that statistically will not exist. Content budget is allocated inversely to where players actually are.

**처방**: Collapse to three visible numbers at launch: silver, one material, and hull progress. Push renown, notoriety, morale, loyalty, voyage credits and the T5 tier behind Gate E — they are all invisible to a player who churns at hour two. Then pull T2 forward hard: the second hull should land at 60-90 minutes, not 5-7 hours, because 'I got a new ship on day one' is the strongest thing this genre has and the doc spends it on hour six.

## 🚫 Boarding — the emotional payoff and the only true competitive differentiator — is a teleport to a fixed 60×24 box with 4 buttons, a wheel-capture timer and 6 rotating sub-objectives. The doc's own validation flag admits the failure mode (track boarding-choice rate after 30 boardings; if sink-choice exceeds 40%, boarding is less fun than a 20% cargo premium).
**실패 시나리오**: Six sub-objectives divided across a 45-second timer becomes a QTE within a couple of hours. If players start choosing 'just sink it' because it is faster, the differentiator is dead, the capture fantasy is dead, and the answer to 'why play this instead of Blox Fruits' evaporates — leaving longer travel and a smaller player count. Also note the sink timer keeps running during the 45-second re-board cooldown, so a failed boarding usually means the target sinks and you take the 30% consolation. The doc calls that a soft failure; players will read losing 70% of a manifest they were shown in advance as a hard punishment for attempting the fun thing.

**처방**: Pause the sink timer during boarding cooldown so a failed attempt is genuinely soft. Then make the arena vary structurally, not just by objective: 3 deck layouts × ship class, with the layout visible in the spyglass beforehand so approach becomes a decision. And instrument the sink-vs-board choice from the very first alpha — if that ratio drifts, you learn it at week 15 instead of month six.

## 🚫 Six readout channels invested in wind (minimap arrow, masthead vane, flag direction, sail flutter shapekeys, surface ripples, no-go-zone audio + vignette) for a 1.44x total span, plus a deterministic 60-90s oscillation, on a phone.
**실패 시나리오**: Nobody in the phone-majority audience is reading a masthead windvane at 30fps. The most expensive readout budget in the doc is spent on the least legible mechanic, while the things that actually need teaching — which aim band does what, whether heavy shot fell off past 60 studs, whether roundshot is even working on an armored frigate at 0.35x — have their primary feedback channel deliberately removed by the no-damage-numbers rule.

**처방**: Cut wind readout to two channels (minimap arrow + sail flutter). Spend the reclaimed budget on making damage legible without numbers: distinct hit-confirm audio per band, per-band impact VFX (canvas tear vs splinter vs crew cry), and a one-time full-screen callout the first time a shot is resisted by armor. Keep the no-numbers rule for the flight-gap tension, but you must replace the learning function it was doing, not just delete it.

## ✂️ 컷 권고
- Level-gating the spyglass to Lv5 — delete the gate entirely, it costs the first session its information layer.
- The 25-second self-sinking cinematic → 4 seconds, skippable after 1. Nothing in this document is more likely to end a mobile session permanently.
- Wind readout channels 2, 3, 5 and 6 (masthead vane, flag direction, surface ripples, edge vignette). Unreadable on a phone for a 1.44x effect; reclaim the budget for damage legibility.
- T5's three sidegrade hulls, the 24 faction-flavoured capstones, and levels 45-60 — all defer past Gate E. A 55-70 hour endgame designed for a 4-6% D30 cohort is content built for players who will not be there. Ship at Lv45 cap as the doc's own validation note already suggests.
- Renown, notoriety, morale and loyalty as launch systems. Four more numbers for a player who cannot yet name what they are working toward. Morale/loyalty in particular add a decay gauge to the crew you were trying to make people love.
- 56 module purchase steps → 30 (drop to 3 tiers). The doc's own session-purchase target already implies this; do it before shipping rather than as a post-launch correction.
- Drifting cargo / wreck debris at 30% event weight → 12%, and make it a sail-through pickup. Never make the most frequent interruption a 10-second stop for a trickle.
- The revenge contract as a launch feature. In the default PvP-OFF experience the thing that sank you is a respawning NPC, so the Zeigarnik hook has no object. It belongs with tier-2 PvP, which the doc itself worries only ~8% will opt into.
- Custom ship nameplate text at launch (199 R$). TextService plus false-positive support load, on a solo dev, for a ★-visibility cosmetic. Ship the 288-combination preset and revisit in season 2 — the doc already suspects this.
- Officer injury recovery on 5/20-minute real-time timers. Wall-clock penalties inside a ~30-minute session read as punishment, not friction; the doc's own validation item hints at 8 minutes and I would go to 90 seconds or make it voyage-scoped.
- One of the two duplicate stat baselines (T1 sloop 3×34 / 3×420 versus Brig 8×45 / 3×1,000 versus a third table at 45 dmg / 6.0s reload / 3×400). Three mutually incompatible tables mean nobody has computed the actual first-hour TTK. Pick one, recompute, and tune from the real first ship upward.

## 🏛️ 반드시 지킬 것
- Already on the ship, already armed, no loadout screen, no queue, no lobby — the single most load-bearing decision in the document. Never trade it for a tutorial.
- The 0.2-1.4s shell flight gap with smoke occlusion, crew shouts and ambient ducking. Genuinely the best-value juice in the doc. Caveat: it fires roughly 200 times per session, so audition the audio for repetition fatigue specifically, not just for impact.
- Cargo manifest revealed by spyglass BEFORE the fight. This converts random loot into a goal the player already feels they own. Highest effect-per-line-of-code in the entire design — which is exactly why level-gating the spyglass to Lv5 is so damaging.
- 0 HP = disabled, not dead, plus a sinking countdown that puts a clock on the board-or-sink decision. This is the loop's actual engine.
- The reward-choice panel (repair / crew / notoriety / fleet) instead of auto-grant. One UI panel buying real authored variety.
- 3 horizontal aim bands selected by vertical drag — the best mobile-native idea in the document. It makes 'shoot the sails to slow them / the waterline to sink them' work with zero extra UI.
- Ships never destroyed; sinking = towing; permanent assets (hull, modules, plans, deeds, cosmetics) never lost. Correct read of the audience. Just fix the 25-second cinematic and the 8-minute tow that sit on top of it.
- Officers as pure data records that cannot be lost to physics, and legendary officers as sidegrades (new verbs) rather than multipliers. This is a real answer to power creep.
- No paid random items at all, with voluntary odds disclosure and visible pity on the free tracks. Both a regulatory dodge and a marketing line. Keep it absolute.
- Opt-in-only PvP with the default OFF, cargo as the only stake, and clan membership explicitly decoupled from PvP flagging.
- Storms where the counter IS the core mechanic (half sail for gusts, bow-on for rogue waves) and reduced render distance doubling as an escape tool. Weather teaching sailing is the cheapest content in the doc.
- Pre-committing the break-even CCU and the rule that disappointing revenue is diagnosed as 'not enough players,' never as 'sell power.' Write it somewhere the team cannot quietly edit.
