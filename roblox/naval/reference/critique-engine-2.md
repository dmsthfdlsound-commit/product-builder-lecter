# 적대적 검증: 엔진 (2차)

> Roblox technical director, hostile review. VERIFICATION CAVEAT UP FRONT: this session's web search budget was exhausted before I could query devforum/docs, so nothing below cites a source. Everything is from established engine behavior and from internal contradictions in the document itself — which is where most of the damage is anyway. Any claim I mark "unverified" needs a 30-minute docs pass before you act on it. I did NOT independently confirm the doc's specific numeric claims (the 10-vehicle rubberbanding threshold, the ImprovedPhysicsReplication open bugs, AuthorityMode GA date, the 64-attribute limit, the 0.05ms/9-plane ocean reference, DevEx 37.8%). Treat all of those as the author's assertions, not facts. The document's real problem is not that its facts are wrong — it's that it describes two incompatible games and never notices.

**판정: needs-rework**

## 🚫 The document specifies two mutually exclusive ship architectures and never reconciles them. Ship §5.1 mandates pure kinematic integration: root Anchored=true, and 'LinearVelocity, AngularVelocity, AlignPosition, AlignOrientation, PlaneConstraint, VectorForce 전부 미사용.' Combat §4.1, Combat §10, and Roadmap S2 all specify dynamic physics: '부력 = 6개 샘플 포인트 VectorForce', '서지 = LinearVelocity', '요 = AngularVelocity', and S2's PASS criterion is '전복 0회' (capsize count) — a metric that only exists for a dynamic rigid body. An anchored kinematic hull cannot capsize.
**실패 시나리오**: These are not two tunings of one system, they are two codebases with different deck controllers, different exploit surfaces, different network ownership, different hit verification, and different buoyancy code. The W0-W2 spike phase as written VALIDATES THE ARCHITECTURE §5.1 FORBIDS. Concrete failure: you spend S2 (5 sessions) proving VectorForce buoyancy is stable, mark it PASS, then in W3 discover §5.1's deck controller (writing shipRoot.CFrame directly) cannot drive a dynamic assembly — CFrame writes to an unanchored root fight the solver and produce constraint explosions. You now rewrite either the hull mover or the deck controller at W3, having burned the spike budget proving the wrong thing. The doc's own S2 fallback text admits this: 'S3 예산이 2배가 된다.'

**처방**: Pick kinematic, before W0. Delete the VectorForce/LinearVelocity/AngularVelocity language from Combat §4.1, Combat §10, and Roadmap S2 entirely. Rewrite S2's PASS criteria for a kinematic body: (a) root CFrame written once per frame via a single Model:PivotTo, (b) 4-point analytic wave sampling drives target Y/roll/pitch through a critically-damped spring on the TARGET VALUES not on forces, (c) replace '전복 0회' with 'roll never exceeds 35° and always returns to level within 3s', (d) add 'zero physics constraint instances exist on the hull' as a hard assertion in the test harness. Buoyancy-as-forces is the wrong tool once you've decided the engine doesn't simulate the hull — you get the pitch/yaw-from-player-jump bug back that §5.1 correctly cites as a reason to avoid physics in the first place.

## 🚫 There is no mechanism for seeing OTHER players correctly positioned on a moving deck. §5.3's controller is explicitly scoped '자기 캐릭터만' (own character only). Remote crew are left to Roblox's default character replicator, which streams world-absolute CFrames.
**실패 시나리오**: This kills the game's single differentiator. A crewmate standing still at the gun station on a 106 studs/s schooner is replicated to you at a world position that is stale by (their latency + your latency + replication interval). At a realistic 150ms round trip that is ~16 studs; at 250ms on mobile it is ~26 studs. On a 88-stud schooner, your crewmate is rendered a third of a ship-length behind the stern, standing on open water, permanently, for the entire voyage. When the ship turns at 28°/s the error becomes lateral and they orbit off the hull. This is precisely the GPO/Blox Fruits boat failure the design claims to be differentiating against, and it is the one failure mode the document never mentions. Every crew station, the 2-6 player capacity, the officer takeover rule in §5-1, the '함께 항해한 사람' stamp cards — all of it renders as other players sliding around in midair next to the boat.

**처방**: Write your own replication path for embarked characters, in ship-local space. Server keeps authoritative ship state in a Lua table. For each embarked player, replicate (shipId, localOffsetVector3, localYaw, stateEnum) at 10-15Hz instead of letting the default replicator send world CFrame. Each client reconstructs remote crew as shipCFrame * CFrame.new(localOffset) * CFrame.Angles(0,localYaw,0), where shipCFrame is that client's own locally-predicted ship transform — so remote crew are stale in ship-local space (a few studs of walking lag, invisible) instead of stale in world space (26 studs of ship travel, catastrophic). You will need to suppress the default replicator for these characters, which in practice means the embarked character's parts are CanCollide-managed and driven client-side from your own stream, with animations played from the replicated stateEnum. Budget this as a 3-4 session task of its own, at W1, BEFORE S3. If you will not build this, cut multi-crew to a single pilot per ship and rebuild the entire crew/station/officer design around solo play.

## 🚫 Writing hrp.CFrame every frame while the character stands on a teleporting Anchored collider is the worst-case input for Roblox's contact solver, and it is budgeted at 6 sessions (W3 / S3).
**실패 시나리오**: An Anchored part moved by CFrame writes has zero AssemblyLinearVelocity (the doc knows this) but also DISCONTINUOUS POSITION — it teleports each frame rather than sweeping. The solver sees the deck collider appear inside the character's capsule with a large penetration depth and applies a correspondingly large corrective impulse. That is the classic moving-platform launch bug. Combined with CFrame writes not updating the character's velocity, the Humanoid state machine oscillates between Running and Freefall as the deck separates and re-penetrates under wave roll (±7° at T1, ±32° during a rogue wave). Concrete failure: your 8-player full-sail-max-turn test produces intermittent flings and Freefall-state loss of walk friction, you cannot hit the '표류 표준편차 < 0.5스터드, 낙수 0회' PASS bar, and you burn the 5-session timebox discovering that a from-scratch moving-platform character controller is not a 7.5-hour job. The doc says this is '이 프로젝트에서 가장 어려운 엔지니어링' and then gives it less time than the ocean.

**처방**: Do not fight this at v1. Ship stations as fixed seat interactions with 1-tap transitions between them — the doc already names this as fallback tier 1 and even notes it may be better mobile UX. Make it the PLAN, not the fallback, so W3 becomes a 2-session seat-transition task instead of a 6-session physics fight. If you insist on free deck movement, the specific technical change is: never write hrp.CFrame against a live collider. Instead (a) make deck collision boxes CanCollide=false, (b) drive the character's vertical position from your own analytic deck-plane query (you already have the hull's exact transform — the deck height under the player is a closed-form solve, no raycast needed), (c) drive horizontal motion by integrating player input in SHIP-LOCAL coordinates and only converting to world for rendering. The character then never touches the engine's contact solver for the deck at all, which is the only version of this that is stable. That is a rewrite of the movement layer, not a hook into GroundController, and it is a 4-6 week task, not 6 sessions.

## 🚫 The <5,000 scene instance budget is arithmetically impossible against the doc's own component specs. Bones are Instances.
**실패 시나리오**: The doc's ocean reference is '평면 9장 × 530본' — 530 bones per plane × 9 planes = ~4,770 Bone instances, consuming 95% of the entire 5,000-instance scene budget before a single ship exists. Separately: crew NPCs are specified as 4 (T1) to 14 (T5) per ship, and the progression section prices them at '6명 × ~12 인스턴스 = 72'. At 12 instances each, a T5's 14 crew are 168 instances inside a 360-instance hull budget that also has to contain the hull, masts, 13 broadside guns, 3 mortars, and 8-16 collision boxes. Then add PartCache pools (64 + 32 + 48 = 144 minimum, and a cannonball 'visual part' with a trail is 2-3 instances each). 8 ships × 250 + ocean bones + pools + islands + harbour + player characters lands somewhere north of 8,000 on the doc's own numbers. Failure scenario: you hit S4 (W1 session 5), read 'instance < 5,000' as a PASS criterion, measure 9,000, and cannot tell whether the ocean, the ships, or the VFX is the problem because all three are over.

**처방**: Three specific changes. (1) Count ocean bones explicitly in S1 session 3 and pick the plane/bone topology to a hard cap of 1,200 bone instances total across all LOD rings — fewer, larger planes with fewer bones and a scrolling normal map for high-frequency detail. (2) Cut per-ship real instance count to 40-60, not 110-395: bake the entire visual hull (deck, rails, masts, gunports, stern castle) into 1-3 MeshParts, keep 8-12 invisible collision boxes, and make gun ports/mortars visual-only geometry in the baked mesh with firing VFX spawned from attachments (Attachments are cheap, separate Parts are not). Note that with a kinematic hull the cost that matters is transform+broadphase churn per part per frame — 8 ships × 250 parts is 2,000 parts re-broadphased 60×/sec, and THAT is what eats your mobile frame, not the ocean. (3) Crew NPCs must stop being animated rigs: cap simultaneously ANIMATED crew at 6 across the whole scene, and render all other crew as static posed meshes swapped per sail-state enum. 28 animated humanoid rigs at close range during a boarding (two T5s) is not survivable on a low-end Android regardless of instance count.

## 🚫 StreamingEnabled will stream out enemy ship models mid-combat regardless of your proxy system, and the absolute rule '추가 포커스는 절대 부여하지 않는다' makes the boarding arena unstreamable.
**실패 시나리오**: ModelStreamingMode=Atomic prevents PARTIAL streaming — it does not prevent streaming. Atomic means the whole ship pops in and out as a unit. Your effective combat band is stated as 320-800 studs against a ~1,024 streaming radius; that is not margin, that is combat happening exactly at the boundary. Concrete failure: you are trading broadsides at 780 studs, the target's stream region flickers as both ships move, and the enemy hull vanishes and reappears wholesale. Your proxy script does not get a say — the engine removed the model. Second failure: boarding teleports participants to a world-fixed arena, but their ReplicationFocus is pinned to their ship root hundreds or thousands of studs away, so the arena geometry is not streamed to them. They arrive in an empty void, and your '인테리어 전환 로드 < 1.5s' PASS criterion measures a stream that never happens.

**처방**: (1) Ships are not streamed content. Either set ModelStreamingMode=Persistent on all hulls, or keep hulls outside the streamed hierarchy entirely and manage their client-side visibility/proxy swap yourself — your proxy system is only sound if YOU own the decision of when a hull is present. (2) Amend the focus rule to: 'exactly one ReplicationFocus per player at any time, reassigned on context change.' On boarding entry, set focus to the arena root; on exit, restore to the ship root. The rule you want is 'never more than one', not 'never reassign'. (3) Add an explicit S4 test: two ships circling at 900-1,100 stud separation for 3 minutes, assert zero model-visibility transitions that were not commanded by your proxy manager.

## 🚫 Hit verification tolerance is '선체 전장 × 0.15 + 12스터드' — 42.75 studs for a 205-stud Ship of the Line — layered on top of unlimited-ammo roundshot. That is a sanctioned aimbot.
**실패 시나리오**: Effective claimable hit volume becomes roughly double the ship's actual cross-section. A trivial exploit: fire at the maximum rate the client will let you, send origin/dir/timestamp aimed anywhere within ~40 studs of the target, and every shot is accepted. Because roundshot ammo is infinite by design and reload is the only limiter, and because the doc does not mention server-side rate limiting against the reload timer, the cheat is 'ignore reload, claim every shot.' The doc's stated reasoning ('200ms 되감기 오차는 ~20스터드. 150스터드 표적에는 여전히 명중이다') confuses two different things: rewind error in the TARGET'S POSITION, which the CFrame history buffer already corrects for, versus tolerance on the CLAIMED IMPACT POINT, which should not exist at all.

**처방**: Never accept a client-asserted hit. Accept only a client-asserted SHOT: (origin, direction, clientTime). Server rewinds all ship CFrames to clientTime via the ring buffer, performs its OWN stepped raycast, and the server's result is the only result — hit, miss, or which segment, computed server-side. The tolerance then applies only to clock skew (clamp clientTime to now-300ms..now) and to origin validity (reject if origin is >5 studs from the server's known muzzle position at that timestamp). Additionally: server-side enforce reload (reject shots arriving faster than the weapon's reload minus 10%), enforce arc (reject shots whose direction is outside the firing arc at the rewound hull orientation — this is free and blocks the most common naval cheat, firing broadsides through your own hull), and enforce a per-player shot budget per second. This is strictly less code than the tolerance scheme, not more.

## 🚫 Feeding workspace:GetServerTimeNow() directly into the wave and wind phase functions, plus branching gameplay on client-computed wave height.
**실패 시나리오**: GetServerTimeNow returns Unix-epoch-scale seconds (~1.8e9). In Luau's float64 this survives, but the moment any part of the wave path drops to float32 — a shader parameter, an EditableMesh vertex write, an Attribute, a serialized value — sin(k·x - ω·t) at t=1.8e9 returns garbage, because float32 has no fractional resolution left at that magnitude. Symptom: 'waves look correct in Studio and on my PC, and are a flat or violently jittering plane on phones.' Nearly impossible to diagnose from that symptom. Second, separate problem: math.sin maps to the platform libm and is NOT guaranteed bit-identical between an x86 server and an ARM mobile client. Visually that is irrelevant. But the design branches gameplay on wave height in at least three places — rogue wave hit/miss ('파고 임계값 + 선수각'), grounding ('수심 < 흘수'), and the submerged predicate that makes below-deck dry. A float that differs by 1e-15 across architectures near a threshold gives you rare, unreproducible desyncs: the server says the rogue wave hit you broadside for 180 damage and your client rendered you taking it head-on for zero.

**처방**: (1) Define a per-place constant GAME_EPOCH and use t = GetServerTimeNow() - GAME_EPOCH everywhere, keeping t under ~1e7 for the game's lifetime. Assert this in the S1 harness. (2) Establish a hard rule: GetHeight is authoritative ONLY on the server for any decision that has a gameplay consequence. Clients call GetHeight for visuals and for predicting their own hull's pose, never for hit/miss, damage, grounding, or submersion. Rogue wave resolution, grounding stage transitions, and flooding are server-computed and broadcast as events. (3) Change S1's PASS criterion from 'max height error < 0.05 stud' to 'max height error < 0.05 stud AND zero gameplay predicates are evaluated client-side' — the first number is a red herring if the architecture lets clients decide anything.

## 🚫 Ship-vs-ship overlap resolution — '서버가 OBB 교차를 20Hz로 검사하고, 겹치면 두 선박의 목표 속도 벡터에 분리 성분을 더한다(임펄스 아님)' — cannot possibly resolve overlaps at these speeds.
**실패 시나리오**: Two ships closing head-on at 106 and 96 studs/s cover ~10 studs of mutual approach per 20Hz tick. Separation is applied to TARGET VELOCITY, which is then reached through an exponential with acceleration time constants of 3.4-11.0s and deceleration 5.5-15.0s. The separation force therefore takes multiple seconds to produce meaningful displacement, during which the hulls interpenetrate by tens of studs. Concrete failure: the design explicitly rewards the highest-skill maneuver in the game — 근접 화력's 「양현 동시발사」, threading between two ships and firing both broadsides — and that maneuver renders as your 152-stud frigate passing THROUGH the enemy hull like a ghost, every single time, in the most screenshot-worthy moment of the fight. Every close pass, every failed ram, and every boarding approach exhibits it.

**처방**: Resolve overlap POSITIONALLY on the server, not through velocity. On OBB intersection, compute the minimum translation vector and directly offset both root CFrames apart along it (split by mass ratio), immediately, in the same tick. Your reconciliation already absorbs sub-12-stud corrections through a 0.15s blend invisibly, and glancing contacts are well under 12 studs. Additionally raise the check rate to 30-60Hz for pairs whose closing speed exceeds 60 studs/s (cheap — it is a handful of OBB tests, and you already know every hull's exact transform), and add predictive separation: if the swept OBBs will intersect within 0.3s, begin the positional correction before contact so it distributes across frames. Reserve visible interpenetration exclusively for the ram, which is a designed 0.2s event with debris VFX covering it.

## 🚫 Boarding invulnerability is exploitable as a cooperative defensive cooldown, and the instant relative-CFrame snap will read as a teleport bug.
**실패 시나리오**: Both hulls are fully invulnerable ('박격포·충각·회오리 전부 무효') for up to 60s, with a 45s cooldown. Two colluding players in 사략 해역 mutually board each other on rotation and are untargetable roughly 60s out of every 105s while a third farms objectives or escapes. The guards written ('연쇄 보딩 차단', '동일 표적 재접선 금지') address one player chaining boardings, not two players alternating. Separately: at closing speeds up to 106 studs/s with 10Hz replication and 60Hz client prediction, snapping the follower hull to a fixed relative CFrame produces an observed positional discontinuity of tens of studs — the doc's own reconciliation treats >45 studs as 'instant snap with spray-burst masking.' A 40-stud sideways hull teleport at the exact moment the player executes the grapple, in the highest-attention moment of the loop, reads as a bug and will be clipped as one. Third: what happens to a third party's mortar shell already in flight?

**처방**: (1) Change invulnerability to damage DEFERRAL: accumulate all incoming damage during the lock in a server-side accumulator and apply it on lock exit, with the segment/flood consequences resolving then. Nothing is nullified, so nothing is farmable. (2) Forbid initiating a boarding while you have taken damage from a third party in the last 8s, and forbid it entirely for a pair that has boarded each other within the last 10 minutes. (3) Replace the instant snap with a 0.6s scripted approach: both hulls lerp along a designed curve to the locked relative pose, with the grapple rope VFX spanning exactly that interval. Cheap, and it converts the worst-looking frame in the game into the best-looking one. (4) Write down the rule for non-participating crew: their ship is frozen and invulnerable — decide whether they can still man the swivel gun (recommend no; give them a spectator camera on the arena instead, which also solves 'what do the other 3 players do for 45 seconds').

## 🚫 The depth field's spatial resolution cannot represent the archipelago it exists to serve. 256×256 over 8,000 studs is 31.25 studs per pixel.
**실패 시나리오**: A T5 Ship of the Line has a 17-stud draft and a 205-stud length, spanning ~6.5 pixels. Any reef, shoal, or navigable channel narrower than roughly 60-90 studs cannot be expressed at all after bilinear interpolation smears adjacent samples. Concrete failure: you hand-author an inter-island channel 70 studs wide as a skill-expression shortcut, the depth field interpolates the island shallows across it, and the grounding stage fires in the middle of open water — or conversely a charted safe channel reads as deep while the visual geometry says rock, and players ground on nothing. Depth VALUE resolution (8-bit over ~200 studs, <1 stud) is fine; the spatial resolution is what fails.

**처방**: 1024×1024 (7.8 studs/px, ~1MB as 8-bit — trivial) as the base field, PLUS hand-placed explicit convex 'shoal' and 'channel' polygons that override the field for any gameplay-critical passage. Query order: channel polygons (force deep) → shoal polygons (force shallow) → interpolated field. Keeps the cheap field query for open water and gives you authored precision exactly where the design cares. Also add to the harness: render the depth field as a debug overlay against the actual island meshes and eyeball every channel — this is a 20-minute tool that prevents a whole class of 'grounded on nothing' bug reports.

Cheaper alternative worth testing in S1: a per-island vertex-shader wave-amplitude falloff driven by a distance-to-shore mask, damping wave height to zero near land — this is how most shipping games do it and it costs one texture per island region.

## 🏛️ 반드시 지킬 것
- Brace: enemy gunport emissive telegraph → hold input → damage reduction, with a perfect-brace window. Near-zero implementation cost (material swap + a server-timestamped flag), it converts an HP race into a duel, its cost model (speed and turn penalty, no firing) makes it self-limiting without a cooldown, and the doc is right that no Roblox naval game does it. Highest juice-per-line-of-code item in the entire document. Build it in W3 as planned.
- Neutralize → board-or-sink as a player DECISION rather than a death, with a sinking timer attaching a stopwatch to it, and the spyglass revealing the cargo manifest BEFORE the fight. This is the loop's actual engine. Pre-revealing the manifest makes the cargo feel owned before it is earned, which turns a random drop into a defended objective. Cheapest depth in the document.
- The reward-choice panel (repair / crew / infamy / fleet induction) instead of automatic granting. One UI panel that makes identical content produce a different story every time and lets the player author their own voyage.
- The 3-state sail machine bound to 6 simultaneous feedback channels via a single enum, with 2.5s transitions and adjacency-only changes. Discrete states make the tradeoff legible, make transitions feel like decisions, cap authoring at 3 presets, and reduce to two mobile buttons. The full-sail-only ram condition keeping full sail combat-relevant is a genuinely good bit of design.
- Weak point generation from accumulated roundshot damage → swivel gun aligned shot, with the top module tier automating it. A skill-expression axis with a measured ~30-35% ceiling that converts mastery into convenience at endgame.
- The absolute no-damage-numbers rule, with debris, list angle, smoke density, and speed loss as the damage UI. Correctly identified as the thing that would kill the +0.2 to +1.4s flight-window dopamine peak.
- Sinking equals towing, never hull loss; loss floor at cargo only; officers cannot permanently die; advancement stamps lose at most 1 on death. The loss-depth ceiling is the strongest product decision in the document and every mechanic respects it consistently.
- Boarding as a world-fixed instanced arena rather than a melee on two rocking decks. The single best technical decision here — it eliminates moving-platform velocity inheritance, cross-owner collision, deck-to-deck jumping, and the crew-swept-overboard bug class simultaneously, and 'two ships lashed together belching smoke with sword SFX' is entirely sufficient for observers. Keep the design; fix the invulnerability model and the snap (blockers 10).
- Zero unanchored gameplay parts. Cannonballs as stepped raycasts with PartCache visuals, fire barrels as anchored parts with server radius checks, floating loot non-physical. This closes the Inf/NaN velocity, teleport, fling, and spoofed-Touched exploit surface by construction rather than by detection.
- Deterministic wave and wind as pure functions of a synced clock with zero replication. Correct and elegant. Needs the epoch subtraction and the server-only-gameplay-decisions rule (blocker 7), but the architecture is right and it is what makes 'dry walkable gun deck' possible — which is a genuinely visible feature competitors using Terrain water structurally cannot ship.
- Damage-driven per-compartment buoyancy target so hull damage produces real list and settling. Highest perceived-production-value per line of code in the document, and it falls out of the 4-point sampling you need anyway.
- Officers as pure data records with visual deck NPCs as massless non-colliding decoration. Makes the entire Arcane Odyssey crew-lost-to-physics bug class structurally impossible, and that is legitimately marketable.
- The power-budget discipline: officer contribution hard-capped at 15% and server-clamped, diminishing returns on stacked effects, legendary as sidegrade-not-multiplier, the contribution gauge shown to the player with its own cap visible, and the veteran-retrofit promise declared AT LAUNCH rather than as later damage control. This is a coherent anti-powercreep system and it is rarer than the document seems to realize.
- The monetization firewall: no paid randomness at all, the six explicit cosmetic-cannot-touch-performance rules (shared collision volume, gunport glow excluded from skin masks, VFX density-and-color only, fixed lantern brightness, event hulls at ±0 stats, 6-free-before-paid slots), the SKU approval decision tree, and the visibility-ordered art priority (hull/sail/flag before VFX before docks before cabin interiors). The reasoning for why cabin interiors sell badly — and why building them first yields the false conclusion that cosmetics do not sell — is exactly right.
- The gate structure with pre-committed quantitative kill criteria, PerfHUD built in session 1 before any gameplay, real-device-only performance evidence, spike timeboxes that auto-fail into a pre-written fallback, and the pre-registered rule that disappointing revenue is diagnosed as too few players and never as a reason to sell power. This is better project management than most funded studios apply, and it is the primary reason this project has a real chance despite everything above.
