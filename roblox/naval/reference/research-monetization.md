## The 2026 toolkit has eight distinct revenue lines, but only three of them will ever matter for a fairness-first RPG.
UNVERIFIED inventory from model knowledge: (1) Passes — one-time permanent, 70% share; (2) Developer Products — repeatable, 70% share; (3) In-experience Subscriptions — USD-priced recurring tiers ($2.99/$4.99/$7.99+), different and worse split because app-store fees apply, requires ID verification; (4) Premium Payouts — pays for engagement time from Roblox Premium subscribers; (5) Creator Rewards — 2025-26 successor/expansion of engagement payouts, reportedly folding in signals beyond pure playtime; (6) Marketplace/UGC avatar items and Limiteds, incl. selling avatar items inside your own experience for a commission; (7) Rewarded video ads and Immersive Ads; (8) Paid Access (Robux entry fee, ~25 R$ min). Enable regional pricing on day one — it is free conversion in your largest-population regions. Treat lines 4-7 as supplements you do not forecast against.

## Robux price psychology: the 3-99 R$ band works because it decouples spending from dollars and fits inside a gift card, not because it is cheap.
Two mechanisms. First, mental accounting is in Robux, not USD — a child evaluating '49 R$' has no dollar anchor, so the pain of payment is severed from the purchase. Second, gift cards arrive in $10/$25 denominations (~800/2,000 R$), so a 49-99 R$ SKU lets a player make 8-40 purchases from one card, and each purchase reinforces the habit. ARITHMETIC on the observed ladder in this repo: 3 R$ nets you $0.0074, 49 R$ nets $0.120, 99 R$ nets $0.243. The 3 R$ tier is not a revenue line — it is a first-purchase conversion device that permanently lowers the barrier to the next tier.

**→ 시사점**: Ship a genuine 3-19 R$ SKU (a single sail dye, one flag) purely to manufacture first purchases, and price your everyday cosmetics at 49/99/199 R$ so they clear from a $10 card. Do not put your cheapest item above 49 R$ — you would be skipping the step that opens the wallet.

Charging a lot for something highly visible and purely decorative is fully compatible with your fairness thesis; charging a little for power is not.

## Ships are the best cosmetic canvas on Roblox — large, visible at distance, persistent, and socially legible — which is the strongest structural argument that cosmetic-only can work here.
Cosmetic revenue scales with social visibility. A hull skin is visible to every player on the horizon and stays visible for the whole session; an avatar accessory is visible at 10 studs for a moment. Suggested SKU map with prices derived from the observed ladder: sail/hull dye 3-19 R$; flag or pennant 25-49 R$; ship nameplate 49-99 R$ (needs TextService filtering — budget moderation cost); figurehead 99-199 R$; cannon VFX (colored smoke, tracer, splash) 99-299 R$; full themed hull skin 199-499 R$; crew outfit set 149-399 R$; port/dock decoration set 99-399 R$; flagship mega-bundle 1,499-2,999 R$. Fleet/guild-wide matching liveries are an underexploited group-purchase mechanic that drives peer spend without touching power.

**→ 시사점**: Build the modular cosmetic system BEFORE the shop: one shared hull mesh with swappable slots for material, decal, sail set, figurehead, VFX, and crew skin. This turns each new SKU from days of work into hours and is the single highest-leverage engineering decision in your whole monetization plan.

## Captain's cabin and below-deck decoration will underperform badly — do not build them early despite the thematic appeal.
Interiors that only the owner sees violate the visibility rule that makes cosmetics sell. Comparable pattern across cosmetic-heavy games: exterior/avatar-visible items dramatically outsell private-space decor, and Roblox's social loop (players see each other's ships in open water and at port) amplifies the gap further. The exception is when the private space becomes public — a visitable cabin, a port-side flagship parade, or a 'flagship of the week' leaderboard board that renders the winner's ship publicly.

**→ 시사점**: Sequence your cosmetic categories by visibility, not by theme: hull/sails/flags first, cannon VFX and crew liveries second, port docks third, cabins last — and only ship cabins once you have a reason for other players to enter them. Spending art budget on cabins before hulls is the most likely way to conclude 'cosmetic-only doesn't work' from bad evidence.

## Time-to-scale is the real threat, not the monetization model: a hypercasual game reaches 10k CCU in days, a naval RPG needs 12-24 months and most never arrive.
This repo's own genre assessment rates RPG/social as '✕ not recommended for solo' specifically because of content-volume requirements, and rates time-to-first-fun for anime/action RPG at 4+ weeks versus 2-3 days for an obby. Also: you literally cannot A/B-test monetization at 200 CCU — there is no statistical power — so all price tuning is a post-scale activity and should not be agonized over pre-launch.

## The minimum acceptable non-cosmetic monetization that does NOT cross into pay-to-win: slots, presets, own-asset information, and PvE-only consumables — with XP boosts as the one genuinely contested line.
Ranked from safest to riskiest. SAFE: extra ship slots and saved loadout presets (Deepwoken precedent); wardrobe/cosmetic storage; fast travel between ports already unlocked; extra trade-route bookmarks; outfit and dye presets; notifications about your own assets; a cosmetic-XP-only season pass accelerator. BORDERLINE: warehouse/bank storage — becomes economic advantage the moment it enables market cornering; cargo capacity is straightforwardly power in a trading game and should never be sold. CONTESTED: XP and currency boosts — community tolerance is high in pure PvE and collapses the moment there is meaningful PvP or contested resources, which a naval game inherently has. FORBIDDEN under your thesis: any combat stat, hull HP, cannon damage, speed, camouflage or hitbox-altering skins, theft immunity, griefing protection, private servers as the only escape from harassment (the SAB anti-pattern this repo documents at 79 R$/mo), trade slots or market access, and undisclosed-odds gacha.

**→ 시사점**: Ship slots + presets + fast travel + own-asset notifications gives you a real non-cosmetic revenue line with zero fairness cost — take all of it. On XP boosts: if you sell any, constrain them to all four of (a) PvE-only, (b) consumable not permanent, (c) also purchasable with free currency so there is an alibi, (d) capped so they compress grind without raising any ceiling. The safer alternative is to sell season-pass XP only, which accelerates cosmetics and nothing else.

## Fairness pays off through cheaper algorithmic distribution and engagement payouts, not through higher ARPDAU — be honest with yourself about which lever you are actually pulling.
Removing gacha and power sales will lower your ARPDAU relative to a predatory comparable; gacha is typically the highest-ARPPU mechanic in the category and direct-purchase shops convert fewer whales (analytical judgment, not measured — do not let anyone claim otherwise with a number). What fairness buys instead is retention, and retention is what Roblox's discovery algorithm rewards with free impressions, plus engagement-based Premium Payouts / Creator Rewards which pay for TIME rather than spend. This repo also identifies a distribution channel competitors cannot access: parent-facing search. 'Published odds, no gambling, no pay-to-win' is a searchable claim that parent-guide sites and YouTube amplify for free — and this repo documents that predatory monetization is precisely what those outlets attack (a former Square Enix exec publicly calling Grow a Garden 'somewhat evil', BBC scam coverage, a UK parliamentary petition on Adopt Me egg randomness).

**→ 시사점**: Underwrite the fairness thesis as a user-acquisition strategy with a secondary retention benefit, not as a revenue-per-user strategy. Concretely: budget for lower ARPDAU and higher organic CCU, and invest the marketing effort you save into being the naval RPG that parent-guide sites recommend. Do not forecast engagement payouts as a primary line — they are a supplement of unverified but commonly-minor magnitude.

## Sustainable live-ops cadence for a small team: 8-week seasons with a weekly rotating shop that is half new and half vault — and it will permanently consume 1-1.75 FTE.
Recommended shape: 8-week season, ~30-50 cosmetic-only pass rewards across free and paid tracks, 1-2 gameplay beats per season, and a weekly shop rotation of 4-6 SKUs where only 2 are new and the rest are returning from a vault. Cost estimate (judgment, not measured): with a modular cosmetic system, one artist produces ~4-8 shippable ship SKUs/month, so a season needs ~10-16 genuinely new assets and the vault supplies the rest. Ongoing load is roughly 0.5-1.0 FTE art, 0.25-0.5 FTE engineering, 0.25 FTE community — meaning a three-person team commits a third to half of total capacity to live-ops, forever. Revenue allocation to model: pass ~30-50%, rotating shop ~30-50%, subscription ~10-20%, engagement payouts and ads single digits. Two Roblox-specific cadence tactics this repo documents as working: title-suffix updates ('| Storm Season') to trigger algorithmic re-exposure, and weekend boost events.

**→ 시사점**: The vault-and-return model is not a nicety — it is what makes the cadence survivable at your headcount, and it simultaneously implements this repo's anti-FOMO stance (announce re-runs rather than punishing absence). Also note that update droughts are themselves a top-10 community grievance affecting 10+ of the top 20 games, so a reliable 8-week rhythm is a differentiation lever, not merely an ops cost. Build the modular system in month one or the cadence will eat the team and no gameplay will ship.

## ⛔ 하드 제약
- SCALE, NOT MODEL, IS THE BINDING CONSTRAINT. Below ~2,000 stable CCU no monetization design of any kind funds a team (~$2-3k/month net at $1.0-1.5 net per CCU/month). Break-even is ~5,000 CCU solo, ~9,000-13,000 CCU for a three-person team. If revenue disappoints at low CCU, the correct diagnosis is 'not enough players', and the wrong response — the one that kills the differentiation thesis — is to start selling power.
- PAID ACCESS WILL NOT WORK FOR A NEW NAVAL RPG. Roblox discovery feeds on visits and playtime; a Robux entry fee starves the algorithm at the top of funnel. Deepwoken is the exception that earned it through pre-existing hype and word-of-mouth. Do not assume you are the exception.
- A ONE-TIME-PURCHASE-ONLY CATALOGUE CAPS LTV AT THE FIRST SALE. You need at least one repeatable SKU (currency pack, season pass, or subscription). Passes alone cannot produce a growing ARPDAU because Roblox revenue is driven by repeat purchases from a small percentage of payers.
- COSMETIC-ONLY WITHOUT A 1,499-2,999 R$ PRESTIGE TIER IS ARITHMETICALLY CAPPED. 137 sales at 2,999 R$ equal 4,122 sales at 99 R$. Refusing high price points is not fairness — it is just leaving the whale segment unserved while keeping all the disadvantages of cosmetic-only.
- LOW-VISIBILITY COSMETICS DO NOT SELL. Captain's cabins, below-deck decor, and anything only the owner sees will underperform badly. Cosmetic revenue is proportional to social visibility. Do not spend art budget here before hulls, sails, and flags exist.
- PREMIUM PAYOUTS / CREATOR REWARDS CANNOT BE A PRIMARY LINE. Engagement-based payouts are a supplement of commonly-minor (unverified) magnitude. Never build a plan whose break-even depends on them.
- REWARDED VIDEO ADS ARE A ROUNDING ERROR BELOW ~50k DAU. Include them to monetize non-payers and because they are fairness-compatible, but forecast them at zero.
- YOU CANNOT VALIDATE MONETIZATION BEFORE YOU HAVE SCALE, AND SCALE TAKES 12-24 MONTHS FOR THIS GENRE. There is no statistical power for pricing A/B tests at a few hundred CCU. Monetization tuning is strictly a post-scale activity; the naval RPG cannot fund itself for at least a year, so it needs an external cash-flow bridge.
- SELLING SAFETY OR PROTECTION IS OFF-LIMITS EVEN THOUGH IT MAKES MONEY. Anti-griefing immunity, theft immunity, or private servers as the only escape from harassment (Steal a Brainrot's 79 R$/month pattern) is the single most direct contradiction of a fairness thesis. Basic protection must be free.
- CARGO CAPACITY, COMBAT STATS, SPEED, HULL HP, AND CAMOUFLAGE/HITBOX-ALTERING SKINS ARE UNSELLABLE UNDER THIS THESIS. In a naval trading and combat game, cargo capacity is power, not storage. Likewise trade slots or market access, which additionally invite the scam economy documented across 12+ top-20 games.
- UNDISCLOSED-ODDS GACHA IS BOTH A REGULATORY AND A BRAND LIABILITY. Loot boxes are the highest-ARPPU mechanic and giving them up costs real whale revenue — accept that cost explicitly. If any randomness ships, it must be cosmetic-only with published odds and an on-screen hard pity counter.
- INFORMATION ABOUT CONTESTED WORLD STATE IS PAY-TO-WIN IN DISGUISE. Blox Fruits' 2,700 R$ Fruit Notifier sells only information yet reads as P2W. Own-asset notifications are safe; rare-spawn or treasure-location notifications are not.
- CUSTOM SHIP NAMEPLATES CARRY UNAVOIDABLE MODERATION COST. Paid custom text requires TextService filtering and generates support load from mis-filtered names. Either price it to cover that or make nameplates preset-based.
- THE MAY 2026 CROSS-GAME SALE BAN BLOCKS PORTFOLIO MONETIZATION. You cannot sell items in game A that are usable in game B — this is what broke Pls Donate's core mechanic. Cross-promotion between your hypercasual titles and the naval RPG must be traffic-only, not commerce.
- LIVE-OPS IS A PERMANENT 1-1.75 FTE COMMITMENT, NOT A LAUNCH TASK. An 8-week season cadence consumes a third to half of a three-person team's capacity indefinitely. Without a modular cosmetic system built in month one, the cadence will consume the entire team and gameplay will stop shipping.

- Fleet and guild cosmetics are underexploited on Roblox. Matching fleet liveries, crew uniforms, and guild flags create group-purchase peer pressure with zero power implications — a naval game is structurally better suited to this than almost any other genre.
- Turn cosmetics into content for free marketing: a public 'flagship of the week' board, port parades, spectacle cosmetics tied to live events. This solves the visibility problem for expensive SKUs and generates the shareable clips that drive TikTok/Shorts acquisition.
- Character and loadout slots (the Deepwoken precedent) are the most defensible non-cosmetic SKU in mid-core RPGs — real revenue, near-zero fairness cost, and mid-core players genuinely want them. Take this line fully.
- Regional pricing is inconsistently enabled by mid-tier developers. Opting in raises conversion in high-population, low-ARPU regions at zero cost and is thematically consistent with a fairness brand.
- Rewarded video ads give the ~97% who never pay a currency trickle — it monetizes non-payers, is fairness-compatible, and reduces the resentment that drives 'this game is pay-to-win' sentiment even in games that aren't.
- Reliable 8-week cadence is itself differentiation: update droughts are a documented grievance across 10+ of the top 20 games (Murder Mystery 2, Blox Fruits with gaps up to 10 months, Doors' multi-year Floor 3 wait). Shipping predictably is a competitive moat a small team can actually hold.
- Horizontal rather than vertical content expansion protects your cosmetic economy's value. Pet Simulator 99 and Toilet Tower Defense both destroyed their own economies through power creep that made owned items worthless — a cosmetic-only catalogue that never obsoletes prior purchases compounds trust and makes each past purchase a reason to keep playing.
